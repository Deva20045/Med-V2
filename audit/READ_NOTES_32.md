## Ch32 Temporal & Occipital Lobe p563–565

- **p563:** Superolateral temporal lobe opens with a Site-of-lesion / Defect table: (1) Auditory cortex (areas 41, 42) — B/L temporal lobe → Deafness (auditory inattention); auditory connection fibres (auditory cortex to Wernicke's area) → pure word deafness; auditory association cortex → auditory agnosia; (2) Wernicke's area (superior temporal gyrus), area 22 → Wernicke's aphasia: Language (Comprehension), no weakness; (3) temporo-occipital association area (visuospatial function) → visual agnosia: inability to identify objects by vision. Auditory, olfactory and gustatory hallucinations are seen in lateral temporal lobe lesions.
- **p563 (medial temporal lobe / limbic cortex):** "most epileptogenic foci in the body". Diagrams: cingulate-cortex loop (cingulate cortex, anterior thalamus, mamillary bodies, medial temporal lobe) and the cerebrum-components figure (cingulate gyrus, hippocampus, parahippocampal gyrus, corpus callosum, fornix, pineal gland, hypothalamus, mamillary body/ant. group of thalamic nuclei, amygdaloid body) with the coronal-section hippocampus label.
- **p564:** Components: superior temporal gyrus, inferior temporal gyrus, parahippocampus, fusiform gyrus. Functions: homeostasis, olfaction, memory, emotion, sexuality. Amygdala plays a role in memory, present on inferior surface; uncus = elevation/bulge of amygdala on inferior surface; herniation affects pupil (3rd nerve palsy). Kluver-Bucy syndrome = AKA B/L limbic abnormality, B/L medial temporal abnormality, B/L amygdala abnormality. Features: hypersexuality; hyperorality d/t affected hypothalamus (feeding, satiety centre); visual agnosia affecting association areas; hypermetamorphosis = visual inattention (need to touch any object to recognise them); decreased fear, increased aggression. Korsakoff's amnestic state: B/L temporal lobe lesion + abnormality in mamillary body.
- **p565:** Apathy — lesion in medial prefrontal cortex and thalamic connections. Occipital lobe W/L (unilateral) lesions: primary visual area (17) → c/L congruent homonymous hemianopia with macular sparing; visual association area (18, 19) = parastriate and peristriate cortex, functions — ocular fixation, fixing size/shape/colour, visual memory; lesion → colour agnosia. Medial-view diagram: calcarine sulcus, parieto-occipital fissure, sagittal sulci, lingual gyrus, primary visual area (Area 17). B/L lesions: Anton's syndrome — vision: cortical blindness (unaware of blindness → confabulate visual description / blame poor vision on dim light / no spectacles); light reflex intact. Colour-vision deficits: colour agnosia, cerebral achromatopsia. Balint's syndrome (B/L occipital lobe lesion): optic ataxia — gauge abnormality (overshoot on finger-nose test); oculomotor apraxia; simultanagnosia — perceives only specific details at a time but not a whole.
