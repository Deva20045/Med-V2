## Book p646 — 04.pdf PDF21 — Ch48 title page "MYASTHENIA GRAVIS"

- Running head `646 | Neurology`; chapter title `MYASTHENIA GRAVIS`; Pathophysiology (00:00:40).
- Neuromuscular junction (NMJ) disorder.
- **Definition:** Autoantibody (B-cell) + T-cell mediated attack on acetylcholine-receptors (AChR) on post synaptic membrane.
- NMJ figure labels: Acetate, Choline, ChAT, Axon, Myelin sheath, V/Q Ca++ channel, Ca+ ions, AChE, Voltage gated Na+ channels, Voltage gated K+ channel, Agrin, Active zone, Voltage gated Ca+ channel.
- Flow: Ach released from vesicle → Binds to AChR on postsynaptic membrane → Causes influx of sodium → Muscle contractions.
- **m/c presentation : Ocular symptoms**
  - No progression of disease (15-20%) → Ocular myasthenia (Good prognosis).
  - Disease progresses (maximum within first 2-3 years) → Pharyngeal muscles → Proximal muscles → Respiratory muscles.
- most crucial period in the disease : **First 1 yr after ocular symptoms**.

## Book p647 — 04.pdf PDF22 — Ch48

- **Demographics :** Bimodal peaking. First peak 20-40 yr, Females >>> males. Second peak >60 years, males > Females.
- Antibodies table: AChR antibody : Specific — 85% generalised MG, 50% ocular myasthenia. Anti-MuSK — 10-20% generalised, 40-50% of anti-AChR Ab (-) MG. Anti-LRP4 — double negative (both anti-MuSK & anti-AChR Ab (-) MG).
- **Anti-MuSK antibodies :** Absent in ocular myasthenia; Females >>> males; Neck extensor weakness (Neck drop); Tongue fasciculations (+); No thymus involvement; Drug (Pyridostigmine) refractory.
- **Thymus:** In 80% seropositive cases → 70% thymic hyperplasia, 10% thymoma. Thymectomy in adult onset generalised MG : very good prognosis.
- **Clinical Features:** Pure motor syndrome; Diurnal variation; Fatiguability; Fluctuating weakness of UL proximal muscles with repetitive movements.
- Early onset (Classical) MG: 65% of MG cases; Females >>> males; 20-40 years; Antibodies (++++); HLA-DR3, B8, DR9; Thymic hyperplasia (+).
- Note: FSHD : Symmetrical ptosis. CPEO : Ptosis + ophthalmoplegia — Chronic progressive external ophthalmoplegia — In mitochondrial myopathy.

## Book p648 — 04.pdf PDF23 — Ch48

- Presenting features: Ocular symptoms m/c — Asymmetrical ptosis + diplopia + ophthalmoplegia; Binocular diplopia; Pupils spared; muscle with maximal involvement : medial rectus (MR). Limb weakness : 10% cases. Bulbopharyngeal: Fatigue while chewing (D/t masseter — Specific for MG); Dysarthria; Dysphagia. Expressionless facies. Drooped posture.
- On examination: Peek sign (eyelid closure weakness); Curtain sign (open ptotic eye → opposite eyelid droops); Cogan's lid twitch (look up after sustained down gaze → eyelid overshoots); Sustained upgaze → Ptosis develops; Unable to count digits 1-10 quickly.
- Investigations: 1 Ice pack — Cold temperature (Improves conduction). 2 Edrophonium 2 mg IV (Cholinesterase inhibitor). 3 Neostigmine 1 mg IM. All three → Symptoms improve. 4 AChR antibody : most specific test. 5 Single nerve fibre EMG (↑ jitteriness) : most sensitive test.
- NCS table: Baseline CMAP Normal (MG) / Reduced (LEMS); 3 Hz RNS Decremental / Variable; High-frequency RNS Incremental / >100% incremental.

## Book p650 — 04.pdf PDF25 — Ch49 title page "AMYOTROPHIC LATERAL SCLEROSIS"

- MOTOR NEURON DISEASE (MND). Pathology: α / γ motor neurons at ventral horn : Anterior horn cell disease.
- Classification: Pure UMN = Primary lateral sclerosis. UMN + LMN = ALS (90% sporadic, 10% genetic copper-zinc superoxide dismutase defect). Pure LMN = SMA; X-linked Kennedy disease; Multifocal motor neuropathy with paraproteinemia.
- Features of ALS. Etiology: Age > 50 years; M > F; Survival maximum 3 years since onset. Pathology degeneration: LMN — AHC and brainstem nuclei (medulla 9, 10, 11, 12); UMN — Corticospinal tract (CST) and Corticospinal tract (CBT) [printed].
- CLINICAL FEATURES: Cognition intact; HMF, ANS, sensory system spared. AHC 1st structure to degenerate. CST: Brisk reflexes; Loss of dexterity/tone. Presentations: Distal asymmetric UL (classically forearm/hand) atrophy/wasting; Fasciculations & cramps.

## Book p651 — 04.pdf PDF26 — Ch49

- Bulbar vs Pseudobulbar Palsy table: Incidence 40% vs 25%; Involvement CN 9-12 vs fibres to 9-12; Lesion LMN vs UMN; General nasal twang regurgitation vs dysphonic (laryngeal > nasal), aspiration risk, brisk jaw jerk, emotional incontinence; Tongue flaccid with fasciculations vs small spastic tongue; Speech bulbar dysarthria → dysphagia vs pseudobulbar dysarthria; Gag absent vs exaggerated.
- Photos: ALS patient with distal UL & LL atrophy; Tongue atrophy in ALS; Thenar atrophy; Thenar > Hypothenar atrophy.
- DIAGNOSIS: Needle EMG polyphasic fasciculations & fibrillations. MRI B/L symmetrical high intensities (MRI : ALS).
- TREATMENT: No effective drugs. Riluzole & Edaravone maybe tried.

## Book p652 — 04.pdf PDF27 landscape — Ch49 end "ALS Variants"

- **X-LINKED SMA** AKA Kennedy's disease. Pure LMN. Trinucleotide repeat disease. Androgen insensitivity. Spinobulbar: Tremor + fasciculations; Proximal wasting = pseudohypertrophy of calf muscles; Tongue wasting + perioral fasciculation.
- **BENIGN MONOMELIC AMYOTROPHY (BMA)** AKA Hirayama's disease. Young male (<20 yrs); Asymmetrical weakness + wasting of UL extremity; Non-progressive. MRI: Impingement of dura (tight dural canal tearing in flexion/growth discrepancy b/w spinal canal & dura). Callout: Atrophy/thinning of C8-T1. Caption MRI : BMA.
- Note **Madras MND:** Bilfacial weakness + ocular involvement + SNHL + asymmetrical atrophy of limbs. Variant of MND. Photos A–D: Finding of Madras MND.

## Book p659 — 04.pdf PDF34 — Ch50 end

- Origin: Vertebral Artery. 2 posterior spinal arteries : Posterior 1/3rd. 1 Anterior spinal artery : Anterior 2/3rd.
- Radicular arteries (Feeder arteries): reinforce at each level (Lower cervical segment and below). Artery of Adamkiewicz/Arteria radicularis magna (Largest) originates near T8-T12 → entire lower half spinal cord. T4-T5 : Less radicular arteries → ↑ Risk of ischemia.
- Arteria Vasocorona: Circumferential anastomotic channels. (Corticospinal tract in watershed area → myelopathy).
- Pathology: 1. Anterior Spinal artery thrombosis — Acute; Generalized atherosclerosis; Sparing of posterior column. 2. Posterior spinal artery injury : Traumatic accidents.
- Ligamentum denticulatum: Extension of pia mater attaching spinal cord on duramater. Compressive myelopathy : Denticulate ligament compresses corticospinal tract.
